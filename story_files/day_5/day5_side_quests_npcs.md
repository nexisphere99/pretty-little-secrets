### Vanessa: The Memory Charm Drill (Extended Training)

**Location**: Lush Latte Cafe, floor and break room
**Trigger**: Morning Option A (cafe shift)

"The kind that teaches you to pay attention." She arranges the cups in a line. "I'm going to assign each cup a drink. You're going to memorize the order. Then I'm going to scramble them and you're going to unscramble them by taste alone."

"By taste." She starts making drinks. Quick, efficient, her hands moving between machines with the mechanical grace of someone who could build a latte in her sleep and has, probably, literally done so. Six drinks. She slides them into the numbered cups without telling me which is which.

"Ready?"

She scrambles the cups. Swaps positions. Spins them. When she's done, the six cups sit in a random order and I have no idea which is which.

"Taste each one. Tell me the number."

I taste the first. Oat milk latte. Sweet, creamy, a little nutty. I think it was number four. "Four?"

"Three. But close." She marks something on a napkin. "Next."

I work through them. I get three right, two wrong, and one that I can't even identify, some specialty thing with lavender and honey that tastes like drinking a garden.

"Four out of six." She tears up the napkin. "Average. We'll do this every shift until you're perfect."

"Why?"

She leans across the counter. Her face is close. The wine-dark lipstick fills my vision. "Because paying attention is a skill, Alex. And the people who pay attention are the people who get what they want." Her eyes drop to my mouth. Stay there. Come back up. "What do you want?"

The question is too big for the cafe. Too big for the counter between us. I can feel it pressing against the walls, looking for more room, wanting an answer that has nothing to do with coffee and everything to do with why my heart is hammering in my chest and why her lipstick is the only color I can see.

"To get better," I say.

"At what?"

The pause stretches. I could say "at coffee." I could say "at my job." I could say any safe thing and she'd accept it and we'd move on and the moment would dissolve into the regular background noise of employment.

"At being what people need me to be," I say.

Vanessa's expression changes. Something behind her eyes opens, a door or a window or a cage, something that lets a different kind of light through. She reaches across the counter and touches my face. Not my collar. Not my jaw. My face. Her palm on my cheek, warm, sure, possessive in a way that has nothing to do with employment.

"That," she says, "is the right answer."

She pulls away. Goes back to the espresso machine. The moment closes like a book being shut, and I'm left standing at the counter with the ghost of her palm on my cheek and the taste of lavender honey on my tongue and the certain knowledge that whatever game we're playing, it stopped being about coffee a long time ago.
