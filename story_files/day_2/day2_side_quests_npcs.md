## Neighborhood NPCs (Day 2 Availability)

### Mr. Henderson (Recurring)

**Location**: Front yard, as always
**Trigger**: Any time Alex leaves the house

Henderson is a metronome. A human clock. I pass his yard on my way out and there he is, hose in hand, Orioles cap in place, the spray of water catching the light in a fine mist that drifts across the sidewalk and hits my ankles.

"Morning, son!"

"Morning, Mr. Henderson."

"Saw your stepmother heading out early. Sharp dresser, that one. My late wife used to say a woman who keeps herself well keeps her house well." He nods at some private wisdom. "Course, she also said a man who waters his lawn at 7 AM is either dedicated or crazy. I told her I'm both."

He chuckles. I chuckle. The exchange lasts fourteen seconds. I keep walking. Behind me the hose whispers against the grass, steady as breath.

#### Day 2 Special Dialogue (if Alex is wearing panties):

No change in Henderson's dialogue. He can't see the panties. But I feel them while he talks to me, the lace shifting against my hip when I stop and pivot to face him. There's a prickling awareness, a live wire of self-consciousness, standing in bright daylight talking to a seventy-year-old man about lawn care while wearing pink lace under my jeans. Henderson sees a normal kid. I know what's underneath. The gap between those two realities is a space I'm learning to live in.

### Ali (Corner Store Encounter)

**Location**: Ali's Corner Store
**Trigger**: If Alex explores town (Afternoon Option B) or runs a personal errand

Ali is doing his crossword when I walk in. He looks up, pen between his teeth, eyes narrowing at the clue he's stuck on.

"Alex! My favorite. Seven letters, 'gradual change in form.' You know this?"

I think. "Morphing?"

"That's seven." He writes it in. Studies it. "Doesn't fit the intersections. I'll come back to it." He caps his pen. "What do you need?"

"Just looking around."

"You always say that. And you always buy a Gatorade."

He's right. I buy a Gatorade. Blue, this time.

"Hey, Ali. You know that shop down the street? Kinky Kitty?"

His mustache twitches. The twitch contains multitudes: amusement, judgment, curiosity, restraint. "The cat shop. Yes. I know it."

"What's it like?"

"I've never been inside." A pause that lasts exactly long enough to be diplomatic. "The woman who runs it comes in sometimes. Tall, dark hair, lots of jewelry. Buys energy drinks and chocolate bars. She told me once that her shop sells 'lifestyle accessories.' I said 'so do I' and pointed at my display of reading glasses. She laughed." Another pause. "I like her. She's honest about what she sells. Most people aren't honest about anything."

He hands me my change. One seventy-five, exact, without looking at the register.

"Ali, the crossword. 'Gradual change in form.' Is it 'evolve'?"

"Six letters."

"Reshape?"

"Seven! Let me check the intersections..." He grabs his pen, writes, nods. "That works. Reshape. Good word." He tears a strip of paper from his crossword page and writes something on it. "Here. Discount. Next Gatorade is on the house."

"You don't have to..."

"I know I don't have to. I want to. You're a good kid, Alex. Remember that, even when things get... reshaped."

He says the last word with a half-smile that suggests he means more than the crossword. I pocket the coupon and leave with a weird feeling in my chest, like Ali sees something coming that I can't.

*Ali Familiarity +2 (now at 3). Side note: Ali's crossword puzzle is a recurring Easter egg. Each day he's working on a different clue, and the answer always subtly foreshadows Alex's journey. Day 1: no clue. Day 2: "reshape." Day 3: "unveil." Day 7: "blossom." Day 14: "persona." Day 30: "surrender." Players who pay attention to Ali's crosswords will notice the pattern.*

### Vanessa (Pre-Introduction, Visual Only)

**Location**: Lush Latte Cafe, visible through windows
**Trigger**: Afternoon Option B (exploring town)

I don't talk to Vanessa today. She exists behind glass, in a world of steam and pastel and the clink of ceramic. But watching her move behind the counter, watching the way she navigates the cafe's narrow spaces with her hips leading, her ponytail swinging, her hands moving from espresso machine to counter to customer with choreographed fluidity, I start to understand what this job might involve.

It's not just riding a bike and delivering coffee. The cafe has an energy. A vibe. Everything in there is soft. Rounded. The colors are muted. The music, even through the glass, sounds like the kind of thing that plays in a spa. And Vanessa, in her fitted polo and her ruffled apron, moves through it like she was poured into the space, like the cafe was designed around her body and her presence.

When she winks at me through the window I feel a jolt that's partly attraction and partly something I can't name. Like she sees something about me that I'm not ready to acknowledge. Like the wink isn't an invitation but a welcome. Welcome to Lush Latte. Welcome to whatever happens next.

On the walk home, I realize I'm going to that interview. I told myself I was still deciding. But Vanessa winked and my body decided for me.

*Vanessa visual introduction logged. If Alex applied for the job yesterday (Day 1, Afternoon Option C), the interview is tomorrow morning. If not, Alex can apply today via email or in-person, pushing the interview to Day 3 or Day 4.*

### Nicolette (Pre-Introduction, Visual Only)

**Location**: Crystal Haven Mall salon, visible through glass
**Trigger**: Afternoon Option B (exploring town)

The salon is an anomaly. The rest of the mall is under construction, shrouded in tarps and barriers, but this one storefront blazes with light. Through the glass I can see the interior: white marble floors, rose gold fixtures, mirrors everywhere multiplying the space until it looks infinite. Three styling chairs in a row, but only one is occupied, not by a client but by the woman herself, sitting cross-legged in her own chair scrolling through her phone.

She's striking. Platinum pixie cut that hugs her skull and makes her cheekbones look sharp enough to slice paper. Dark eyes ringed with precise eyeliner. Lips painted a deep burgundy. She's wearing a black smock over something silky and her nails are long and coffin-shaped and painted a color that shifts between purple and green depending on the angle.

She looks up. Sees me standing outside her window. Her mouth curves slowly, like a cat spotting a bird. The finger wave she gives me is so delicate, so precise, so intentionally feminine that it feels like a language I don't speak. Each finger curls independently, a ripple of motion from pinky to index, the gesture of someone whose hands are instruments of art.

I wave back with my whole hand, flat and dumb, the wave of someone whose hands are instruments of nothing.

She laughs behind the glass. I can't hear it but I can see it, the way her shoulders shake and her teeth flash white against the burgundy lips. It's not mean laughter. It's delighted. Like I'm the most entertaining thing she's seen all day.

I add her to my mental map. Salon woman. Platinum hair. Mall. She goes into the same drawer as Sophia and Claire and Vanessa, these women who orbit my life at varying distances, each one radiating something specific, each one pulling me toward her with a gravity I don't fully understand yet.

On Day 8, when the mall reopens, I'll walk into that salon for the first time. Nicolette will be behind her chair with a pair of shears in her hand and that same predatory smile. "I've been waiting for you," she'll say. And she'll mean it in every possible way.

## Town Location Scouting

### Kinky Kitty Sex Toy Shop (Exterior Observation)

**Trigger**: Afternoon Option B (exploring town)

The blacked-out windows say everything and nothing. The neon cat winks in an endless loop, hot pink against the dark glass. The "Help Wanted" sign is handwritten in purple ink on card stock, the handwriting elegant and confident with deliberate flourishes on the capital letters.

I stand on the sidewalk and look at this building and try to imagine myself walking through the front door. The image won't form. It's like trying to picture yourself on the moon. Theoretically possible. Practically unthinkable.

But the sign said "discreet" and "open-minded." And the pay is probably better than cafe delivery. And there's something about a shop with blacked-out windows that speaks to the part of me wearing pink panties under my jeans, the part that's starting to learn new words like "Yes, Ma'am" and "I'll be good," the part that's growing in the dark like a plant someone forgot to check on.

I keep walking. But I look over my shoulder once. Twice.

The neon cat winks.

### Lush Latte Cafe (Exterior Observation)

The cafe's Instagram, which I pull up on my phone while standing across the street like some kind of coffee-shop private investigator, tells me things the windows can't.

Their feed is carefully curated. Every photo has the same warm filter, the same pastel palette. Latte art shaped like hearts and leaves and once, inexplicably, a cat. The staff photos are particularly interesting. Three women, all in the same uniform: pastel green polo, black skirt or tight pants, ruffled apron. Their hair is always done. Their makeup is always present. They smile with their whole faces.

And there, buried in a post from two months ago, a photo of a delivery rider. Male, probably mid-twenties, on an e-bike with a branded thermal bag. He's wearing the pastel green polo, and in the photo his hair is pushed back with a headband and the polo is so tight across his chest that it might as well be painted on. The caption reads: "Our delivery cuties keep your caffeine coming! Order through the app for free delivery."

Delivery cuties.

I close Instagram and put my phone in my pocket and stare at the cafe's mint-and-blush facade and I think about tomorrow morning's interview and I think about the word "cuties" and I think about what I'm walking into and whether the front door of Lush Latte is actually a portal to somewhere I'm not ready to go.

*Cafe Instagram observation logged. Alex is now aware of the uniform aesthetic. On interview day, his reaction to the polo uniform will reference this prior scouting.*

## Environmental Side Quests

### Home Exploration: Evelyn's Closet (Glimpse)

**Trigger**: If Alex does Evelyn's morning task and lingers upstairs

After organizing the linen closet, I pass Evelyn's bedroom on the way to my own. Her door is open. She's downstairs. The room sits there, empty, perfumed, full of sunlight filtering through sheers.

I don't go in. Not fully. But I stop in the doorway and look.

Her walk-in closet is visible from here. The door is ajar, a sliver of darkness beyond it that catches the hallway light and throws it back in the sheen of hanging fabric. I can see the edge of something dark, a row of dresses packed tight on a rail, their hems hanging at different lengths. Something white and filmy, a blouse or a slip. The glint of shoes arranged on a shelf, heels of varying heights, all polished, all positioned heel-forward like soldiers at attention.

And on the back of the closet door, visible through the crack, a thin riding crop hanging from a hook.

It could be decorative. Some people mount swords on their walls and have never cut anything more dangerous than a steak. A riding crop in a closet doesn't mean anything. It's an accessory. A leftover from an equestrian hobby. An antique she inherited. There are a hundred innocent explanations.

But I stand in that doorway and I look at that crop and something in my stomach drops, not with fear but with a kind of gravitational awareness, the feeling you get standing at the edge of a high place, looking down, knowing you could fall, knowing the fall would be terrifying, knowing some part of you wants to jump.

I go to my room. I close my door. I stare at the ceiling.

Evelyn's riding crop stays behind my eyes like an afterimage of the sun.

### Home Exploration: Alex's Wardrobe Audit

**Trigger**: Automatic internal narration during any home scene

I open my closet after getting dressed and really look at it for the first time in months. Not to find something to wear but to see what's there. To take inventory, like I'm cataloging a museum collection, except instead of artifacts it's the physical evidence of who I am.

Six t-shirts. Three black, two grey, one blue that Chloe bought me because she said the color matched her eyes, which it doesn't. Three pairs of jeans, all basically identical, varying only in how faded they are. Two pairs of shorts, one athletic, one cargo. A flannel shirt I wear when I want to feel "dressed up." Sneakers that need replacing. Flip-flops for the shower. A blazer from a wedding two years ago that's too tight across the shoulders now.

The most feminine thing in this closet is the blue t-shirt, and that's only because Chloe chose it.

I look at this collection of fabric and I think about Evelyn's closet. The density of it. The variety. Dresses and blouses and skirts and lingerie and shoes for every occasion and accessories I don't have names for. A wardrobe that contains multitudes. Mine contains about six variations on the same theme: guy who doesn't care.

I close the closet. There's nothing wrong with not caring about clothes. Lots of guys don't. It's normal. It's fine.

But I notice, for the first time, that "not caring" feels less like a choice and more like a limitation. Like I never considered the alternatives because the alternatives never presented themselves. And now they're presenting. One pink pair of panties at a time.

*Wardrobe audit logged. Alex's internal awareness of his limited masculine wardrobe is established. This creates narrative contrast for every future clothing acquisition. When Chloe gives him a skirt, when Evelyn presents the maid outfit, when Nicolette holds up a dress, the contrast with "six t-shirts and three pairs of jeans" makes each addition more impactful.*

### Tara (Distant Foreshadowing)

**Trigger**: If Alex checks social media at any point during the day

Scrolling Instagram in bed, I pass a story from an account I barely remember following. "@tara_marie_xo" someone from my high school. Tara Morrison. We were lab partners sophomore year. She had braces and bangs and a laugh like a smoke alarm.

Her story shows her at a club, neon lights behind her, a drink in her hand, her mouth open in a laugh that hasn't changed since high school. She's grown into her face since then. Sharp jaw, dark eyes, confident posture. She's wearing something strappy and revealing and she looks like she owns the room.

I tap to the next story. A group shot. Tara and three other women and, in the background, blurred by movement, a tall Black man in a fitted shirt. He's out of focus but even blurred you can tell he's big. Broad shoulders, narrow waist. His hand is on someone's hip but the crop cuts off whose.

I swipe past. Keep scrolling. Don't think about it.
